2. What do some of the capitalized terms mean in the Agreement?

"Blockchain" means a mathematically secured consensus ledger such as the Ethereum Virtual Machine, the Solana Virtual Machine, any compatible validation mechanism with such a virtual machine, or other decentralized validation mechanisms.

"Transaction" means a change to the data set through a new entry in the continuous Blockchain.

"Smart Contract" means a piece of source code deployed as an application on the Blockchain which can be executed, including self-execution of Transactions as well as execution triggered by 3rd parties.

"Token" means a digital asset transferred in a Transaction, including BTC, ETH, ERC20, ERC721, ERC1155, SOL, and SPL tokens, or other similar tokens and standards using a Blockchain.

"Wallet" means a cryptographic storage solution permitting you to store cryptographic assets by correlation of a (i) Public Key and (ii) a Private Key, or a Smart Contract to receive, manage and send Tokens.

"Recovery Material" means a series of secret words used to generate one or more Private Keys and derived Public Keys, and any additional entropy necessary to uniquely recreate such Private Keys and derived Public Keys.

"Public Key" means a unique sequence of numbers and letters within the Blockchain to distinguish the network participants from each other.

"Private Key" means a unique sequence of numbers and/or letters required to initiate a Blockchain Transaction and should only be known by the legal owner of the Wallet.

3. What are the Services offered?

Our services ("Services") primarily consist of enabling users to create their Quip Accounts and ongoing interaction with it on the Blockchain.

1. "Quip Account"

A Quip Account is a modular, self-custodial (i.e. not supervised by us) smart contract-based wallet not provided by Postquant. Quip Accounts are open-source released under GNU AGPL-3.0.

Smart contract wallet means, unlike a standard private key Wallet, that access control for authorizing any Transaction is defined in code. An example are multi-signature wallets which require that any Transaction must be signed by a minimum number of signing wallets whereby the specifics of the requirements to authorize a Transaction can be configured in code.

Owners need to connect a signing wallet with a Quip Account. Quip Accounts are compatible inter alia with standard private key Wallets such as hardware wallets, browser extension wallets and mobile wallets that support WalletConnect.

2. "Quip App"

You may access Quip Accounts using a Quip web app, mobile app for iOS and android, or browser extension released and signed by Postquant (each a "Quip App"). The Quip App may be used to manage your personal digital assets on Ethereum and other common Blockchains when you connect a Quip Account with third-party services (as defined below). The Quip App provides certain features that may be amended from time to time.
